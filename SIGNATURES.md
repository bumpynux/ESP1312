# Where the signatures come from

Every row in `src/signatures.h` is listed here with where I got it and a rough idea of how much I trust it. Most of the info was lifted from open detector projects or looked up in public registries, and a few rows haven't been checked against real hardware at all. Please open a PR if you have any corrections or suggestions!

## The three kinds of identifier

**OUI.** The first three bytes of a MAC address, assigned to a manufacturer by the IEEE. Anyone can look one up at [maclookup.app](https://maclookup.app/) or in the raw list at [standards-oui.ieee.org/oui/oui.txt](https://standards-oui.ieee.org/oui/oui.txt). An OUI only helps when a device uses its real address. In-car gear (routers, dash cam recorders, MDT WiFi) does. Phones and most body cams randomize, so the OUI rows for those are mostly there for the odd unit that doesn't.

**Bluetooth company ID and 16-bit service UUID.** Numbers a vendor registers with the Bluetooth SIG, published in the [Assigned Numbers document](https://www.bluetooth.com/specifications/assigned-numbers/) at bluetooth.com. These ride inside the advertisement and survive MAC randomization, which is why they matter more than OUIs for wearables.

**Names and manufacturer data.** Device names and the bytes after the company ID in manufacturer-specific data. I couldn't find these registered anywhere, so these come from capturing actual packets.

## Things I chose not to add

- **Flock's module OUIs.** The flock-you project lists 31 more prefixes seen on Flock cameras. They belong to WiFi module vendors like Liteon and would fire on laptops and other gear too. Worth adding once someone confirms which ones matter.
- **Amazon and Google company IDs.** Ring and Nest advertise under their parent's number, which every Echo, Kindle, and Pixel also uses.
- **Sonos OUI `00:0e:58`.** One widely copied list attributes this to Motorola ALPR. It's [registered to Sonos](https://maclookup.app/macaddress/000e58), so adding it would flag every Sonos speaker in earshot.
- **Motorola Solutions.** No Bluetooth company ID I could find, so APX radios and their body cams can't be matched over BLE yet.
